For Susan Dolan, Lincoln Park Pilates

Pitch notes

The short version

Lincoln Park Pilates has a 4.9 on Google, a full weekend schedule and reviews that name the instructors. The website is the one part of the business that undersells all of that. It still carries the Heat 3.0 name, breaks on a phone, hides its schedule and shop from Google, has a dead link behind its main button, and sends every visitor to WellnessLiving the moment they try to book or buy.

This concept keeps WellnessLiving for payments and memberships and puts a front door on it that works the way the biggest reformer studios' sites do. The tab shows which ideas came from where.

1
Booking starts on the site, two ways.

The real weekly timetable, with filters for class, instructor and time of day, spots left, and a waitlist. Every Book and Buy button opens the same panel with a choice. Quick sign-up asks for a name, email and mobile, with no account or password, and holds the spot. WellnessLiving sends members, and anyone who wants to pay by card, straight to the right page of the studio's WellnessLiving schedule or store. It is the pattern Jason built for Hammer Fitness and Boxing in Old Town, where the platform is Mindbody.

What the biggest reformer studios do

This design started with the home pages of five studios that are plainly doing something right: solidcore (about 130 studios), JETSET Pilates (more than 400 franchise territories sold), SLT (21 studios), New York Pilates (nine studios, 1,000+ five-star Google reviews) and Heartcore (eight studios in London). They look different from each other, but they all make the same handful of decisions. This concept makes the same ones.

1
A full-width photo, a few words, one button.

solidcore opens with "Stronger for it." and Book now. New York Pilates opens with "Feel Yourself" and Book class. Nobody opens with a paragraph.

Here: "Reformer Pilates on Southport" and Book a class, over the studio's own photo of two people mid-plank on the reformers.

2
First-timers get the first slot in the menu.

solidcore and JETSET call it "New clients", SLT "First timers", Heartcore "Get started". All five keep the menu to about five items and pin a Book button at the right.

Here: "New here" comes first, the menu has six items, and Book a class never leaves the screen.

3
The intro offer sits above everything.

SLT runs "Special intro offer: get 3 classes for $60" in a bar above its menu. New York Pilates scrolls "Your intro offer is here" across the top and makes intro pricing the second thing on the page.

Here: the red bar on every page and the red $39 block on the home page. It appears twice on the home page, not five times.

4
Proof in one line.

New York Pilates puts five stars and "1,000+ verified 5-star Google reviews" directly under its headline.

Here: "4.9 on Google from 162 reviews" in the same spot, and again as the heading over three quotes.

5
Short sections that each do one job.

solidcore's and JETSET's home pages are about four screens long on a laptop. Each section has one idea and one button.

Here: every home page section has a single heading and a single next step.

6
One brand colour, used for buttons and one big block.

solidcore's blue, SLT's turquoise. The photographs supply everything else.

Here: the red from the studio's logo, with white, black and one pale grey. The photographs are black and white, so red is the only colour on the page.

7
Where a one-studio site can beat the chains.

The chains have to send you to a studio picker before they can show a schedule. Lincoln Park Pilates has one address, so the home page shows today's classes straight under the photo, each with its own Book button.

Why it looks like this

  • Headlines are set in Big Shoulders, the typeface made for the City of Chicago's own design system. It's free, it's tall and condensed like the lettering on a lot of Chicago signage, and it suits a studio named after a Chicago neighbourhood.
  • Photos are the studio's own, from the current site, converted to black and white. That makes the phone shots and the professional shots read as one set, and it matches the black and red of the logo. A half-day shoot would lift the whole site.
  • Shape: anything you can press is rounded and everything else is square.
  • Lists, not cards. Classes, prices and facts are set as plain rows with a rule between them, the way a timetable is, so the page reads as a schedule you can act on.

How this becomes the real site

Same approach as the Hammer Fitness and Boxing site Jason built in Old Town this fall: a fast static site on the studio's own domain, WellnessLiving kept for payments, memberships and the member app, and a small staff portal for everything that isn't a WellnessLiving transaction.

Week 1
Content lock with Susan

Confirm every price and term against WellnessLiving, class descriptions, which formats still run, instructor list and photos, front-desk hours, the intro offer. Get Wix and domain access (the domain stays where it is; only the DNS changes).

Week 2
Build, booking and the staff portal

Quick sign-ups, requests and messages are saved to the site's own database and shown in the staff portal at /admin, behind a staff login, as on the Hammer site. Each quick sign-up is also pushed into WellnessLiving as a lead profile so nobody retypes it. WellnessLiving's Zapier integration has a Create Lead Profile action for this, and the studio's current site already embeds a WellnessLiving lead form. The WellnessLiving buttons link to the exact store item or to the schedule.

Taking payment inside this design, with no hand-off, needs the WellnessLiving API. WellnessLiving publishes an SDK, but it issues the credentials for the studio's account itself. Third-party API directories describe an approval step with a questionnaire and an API agreement, so plan it as a second phase and ask the studio's account executive early.

Week 3
SEO, redirects, launch

Remove noindex, write titles and descriptions, add LocalBusiness schema, redirect every old Wix URL (/copy-of-pricing → /faq and so on), point the domain at the new host, confirm HTTPS, fix the Google Business Profile hours and link it to the new site. Wix stays live until the new site is confirmed, then the Wix plan is cancelled.

After
Hand-off

A one-page guide to editing prices, the schedule and the FAQ, and a monthly check that the intro offer, promo code and prices still match WellnessLiving.

What Susan provides

  • Domain and Wix logins (or add Jason as a collaborator)

Assumptions in this concept

Things that were decided to make the demo complete. Each one is easy to change.

  • The schedule is the WellnessLiving timetable for the week of November 2–8, 2026, treated as a repeating weekly pattern. Wednesday 7:00 am is shown as Rachel's class; it was marked as a sub that week.
  • Spot counts are real for Friday through Sunday (as shown on November 6) and illustrative for Monday through Thursday, which were already past when the schedule was captured.
  • Class descriptions are drafts. The current site describes Flow ("traditional Pilates technique"), Power ("athletic resistance Pilates") and Form & Focus ("traditional moves, all levels"), and those are kept close to the original. Reformer Sculpt and Intro have no description anywhere. Intro's is written from the name and from the FAQ's advice to start with Intro, Flow or Form Focus. Sculpt's says only how often it runs and how long it is, and needs a real description from Susan. Power is marked "some experience"; the site doesn't give it a level.
  • Barre, mat, yoga and stretch classes are not shown as bookable because none appear on the WellnessLiving schedule. If they run, they go on the schedule and the class guide in an hour.
  • Prices follow WellnessLiving where the site and WellnessLiving disagree: 10 classes at $380, the 20-class membership at $289 with a 3-month minimum. The 16-class membership and Elite annual plan are shown because they're in the catalog. Pack expiry and membership minimums follow the WellnessLiving store as checked on November 11, 2026: 5 classes within 3 months, 10 within 90 days, 20 within 180 days, and a 3-month minimum on every membership except Unlimited. The studio's current website says 30 and 45 days for the 5 and 10 packs, so one of the two needs correcting. The "$360 with 2 sauna sessions" version of the 10-pack and the "annual contract" version of the 20-class membership are left out until Susan says which is current.
  • The intro offer is "3 classes for $39", from the WellnessLiving catalog's 2026 Intro Pack. NEW25 is kept as a promo code and can be entered on a drop-in booking or a class pack. The homepage pop-up's "buy 5, get 5 free for $190" is not shown because its terms aren't published anywhere. If only one offer should exist, pick one and the rest come out. Whether NEW25 can be used on the intro pack isn't stated anywhere, so the demo doesn't offer a promo field on it.
  • Rooms are shown as WellnessLiving lists them: two reformer classes a week run in "Studio 2" even though the site calls Studio 2 the barre and mat room.
  • Capacity is 12 per class, per WellnessLiving; the FAQ says 10.
  • Minimum class size is stated as "three or fewer may be cancelled", from the footer; the FAQ says under five and WellnessLiving says fewer than three.
  • The referral reward is written as "$100 off, applied on their second month"; the FAQ's wording is ambiguous about whose month gets the credit.
  • The team lists the eight instructors on this week's schedule. Reviews also mention Peggy and Eila; the homepage says "18 trainers". Add whoever still teaches.
  • Hours are derived from the first and last class each day. Front-desk hours aren't published; the Google profile's hours disagree with the schedule (Tuesday "closed").
  • Transit says the Clybourn Metra and Armitage Brown Line stops are "walkable", from the map. No walking times are claimed.
  • There is no map in this concept, only a Get directions button that opens Google Maps. The real site would embed the map next to the address.
  • Photos are the studio's own, taken from the current site and converted to black and white. The real build should use the originals at full size. Photos showing the old Heat signage were left out or cropped.
  • The sauna has no photo. The one on the current site looks like a stock image, so the sauna is described in words until there is a real picture of it.
  • Sue or Susan. The current site says "Text Sue" and the schedule lists Susan. The concept uses Susan throughout and can switch to whichever she prefers.
  • Instructor photos don't exist on the current site, so instructors appear by name only. Headshots would be worth an afternoon.
  • Grip socks are mentioned under the shop list but can't be ordered online, because the FAQ says they're sold and never says for how much.
  • Per-class figures on the pricing page are simple division (price over classes) and assume every class is used. The Elite annual is divided over 240 classes, twenty a month for twelve months.
  • Private training packs (five and ten 30-minute sessions) exist in WellnessLiving without a visible price, so each has an Ask us button that sends a request for the price.
  • Quick sign-up holds a spot before payment. The concept assumes the studio will hold a spot for someone who has left a name and email, and take payment at the desk or by a link. If Susan wants payment first, quick sign-up still captures the person and then hands off to WellnessLiving checkout.
  • The WellnessLiving buttons are real links. They open the studio's live WellnessLiving schedule and store in a new tab, and anything bought there is real. Most memberships and packs link to their own store page. The intro pack, the 16-class membership, the Elite annual, training and sauna link to the store's front page until their store IDs are confirmed.
  • The staff portal is a demo. It keeps its data in the browser it is opened in, so a sign-up made on a phone will not appear on a laptop. It starts with six people tagged Sample, which can be removed. The real portal uses a shared database and a staff login.
  • Nothing else here is live. Quick sign-ups, purchases and forms are simulated and never ask for card details. They appear in the staff portal so Susan can see what the front desk would receive.
